PERMUTATION-MACHINERY SELF-CHECK (before trusting any p-value below)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Built a synthetic 100-trade / 10-day frame where day-0 losses are 4-6x the
magnitude of every other day's losses, by construction. top3_day_share() on
this synthetic frame = 76.8%. Permuting the pnl vector against the fixed
day-count structure 1000x gives a null with mean=50.6%, p95=60.0%, max=68.9%
-> real 76.8% has p=0.0000. The machinery correctly flags a known-concentrated
case as extreme; it is not just returning "significant" for everything (a
second synthetic leg with no built-in concentration gave observed vs. null
p=0.030, i.e. unremarkable, not near-zero). Trusted for use below.

CLAIM 1: "Wednesday underperforms" (n=36, win 33.3% vs 36.1% overall,
skip-filter saves Rs 1,233 in-sample)
======================================================================
ATTACKS RUN:
1. Multiple-comparisons permutation: shuffled the 5 weekday labels across
   all 144 trades 1000x (weekday bucket sizes held fixed at their real
   counts: Tue 46, Wed 36, Mon 28, Thu 20, Fri 14), and asked how often the
   WORST of the 5 resulting weekday win-rates is <= Wednesday's real 33.3%.
   Result: P = 0.993. On avg_pnl, same test gives P = 0.945 (null worst-
   weekday avg_pnl mean=-55.5 vs Wednesday's real -34.2). With 5 weekdays
   tested, getting a weekday at least this bad by pure chance is close to
   certain — Wednesday's numbers are not even unusual among 5 random
   buckets, let alone real evidence of a Wednesday effect.

2. Per-calendar-date drill-down. There are only 2 calendar Wednesdays in
   the whole 144-trade sample:
       2026-07-22: n=14, win_rate=42.9%, avg_pnl=+5.05, total_pnl=+70.7
       2026-07-29: n=22, win_rate=27.3%, avg_pnl=-59.24, total_pnl=-1,303.3
   The entire "Wednesday" loss (-1,232.7) is more than fully explained by
   ONE date, 2026-07-29 (-1,303.3); the other Wednesday was net positive.

3. Decisive methodological finding: the autopsy's own split-half stability
   check for Wednesday reports half1 n=14 (effect -0.2%) / half2 n=22
   (effect -2.7%), "STABLE (both -)". Those n's are not a random split —
   they are EXACTLY the trade counts of 2026-07-22 (14) and 2026-07-29 (22)
   respectively. Verified: chronological half1 = trades 1-72 covers all of
   07-21/22/23/24 and part of 07-27, i.e. all 14 of 07-22's trades and none
   of 07-29's; half2 = trades 73-144 contains all 22 of 07-29's trades. With
   only 2 calendar Wednesdays total, "split-half stability" for this cell is
   mathematically forced to equal "compare Wednesday-A to Wednesday-B" — it
   cannot detect instability because there is no other way to split a
   2-instance sample. The "STABLE (both -)" label is not evidence of a
   repeatable day-of-week effect; it is a restatement that both of the two
   available Wednesdays happened to be slightly negative (one barely,
   -0.2pp; one badly). This is the SAME underlying event (2026-07-29) that
   also drives claim 4's top-3-worst-days list below — not independent
   evidence, one bad day counted twice under two different labels.

VERDICT: ARTIFACT. Explained away by (a) 5-way multiple comparisons making
a weekday this "bad" a near-certainty by chance (P=0.99), and (b) the whole
effect collapsing to a single calendar date, 2026-07-29, which is not a
Wednesday-specific phenomenon at all — it is a bad trading day that would
look identical if it had fallen on any other weekday.

CLAIM 2: "BUY has stably higher win rate than SELL, but worse avg P&L"
======================================================================
ATTACKS RUN:
1. Permutation test on the win-rate gap itself: shuffled the direction
   label across all 144 trades 1000x. Real gap = BUY 38.9% - SELL 34.4% =
   +4.4pp. Null |gap| distribution: mean=6.7pp, p90=13.3pp. Two-sided
   P = 0.725. The observed 4.4pp gap is unremarkable noise; a same-or-larger
   gap appears in ~73% of random relabelings.

2. Single-worst-trade fragility: worst BUY trade is id=117, ADANIPORTS,
   cci_divergence, stop_loss, pnl=-678.6 (over 40% of BUY's total loss
   deficit vs SELL by itself). Removing it:
       BUY (all, n=54):      win%=38.9%  avg_pnl=-30.0   (BUY-SELL gap=-18.5)
       BUY minus 1 (n=53):   win%=39.6%  avg_pnl=-17.8   (gap=-6.2)
       BUY minus 3 (n=51):   win%=41.2%  avg_pnl=-8.1
   One trade explains roughly two-thirds of the "worse avg P&L" gap; three
   trades erase it almost entirely (avg_pnl -8.1 vs SELL's -11.6 — BUY would
   then look BETTER, not worse). This is not a stable structural payoff
   difference, it is a couple of large stop-outs.

3. Per-strategy breakdown: the BUY win-rate edge is NOT uniform.
       bb_mean_reversion:  BUY 40.9% vs SELL 27.8%  (BUY higher)
       cci_divergence:     BUY 46.7% vs SELL 37.5%  (BUY higher)
       choppiness_filter:  BUY 33.3% vs SELL 40.0%  (BUY LOWER)
       adx_filter:         BUY 0.0%  vs SELL 50.0%  (BUY LOWER, n=2 each, noise)
   Two of four strategies go the opposite direction. The pooled "BUY beats
   SELL on win rate" is an artifact of aggregating strategies with opposite
   internal effects, not a market-wide directional bias.

4. Original stability rail itself was weak on inspection: half1 effect was
   only +0.5pp (essentially zero) and half2 was +6.2pp — "STABLE (both +)"
   only required same sign, not a meaningful, non-trivial effect in both
   halves.

Note the claim is also internally self-defeating even if real: "BUY wins
more often but loses more money" is not an actionable rule (you cannot
"trade BUY" and expect a P&L edge from it; the payoff direction contradicts
the win-rate direction).

VERDICT: ARTIFACT. Permutation test says the win-rate gap is indistinguishable
from chance (P=0.73); the P&L-worse claim is driven by 1-3 outlier stop-outs
concentrated in one strategy/symbol, not a structural BUY/SELL payoff
difference; and the win-rate direction itself flips sign across half of the
four strategies that were pooled to produce it.

CLAIM 3: "cci_divergence trades that exit end_of_day are winners"
(n=21, 52.4% win, +14.0 avg_pnl)
======================================================================
PRIMARY ATTACK (definitional, stated plainly as instructed):
exit_reason is determined by the OUTCOME of the trade, not by anything known
at entry. An end_of_day exit means, by construction: price touched neither
the stop_loss nor the take_profit level at any point during the entire
holding period. That is a fact about the trade's ENTIRE forward path,
resolved only at 15:05 when the position is force-closed. There is no
entry-time rule that can pre-select "the trades that will not hit their stop
or target today" — you only learn that a trade qualifies by having already
lived through the whole session without it happening. Conditioning on
exit_reason is selecting survivors after the fact. Even if this cell were
statistically bulletproof, it is not implementable as a trading rule: you
cannot enter more/fewer end_of_day-exit trades, because "will be an
end_of_day exit" is not observable at entry.

VERIFICATION (is there ANY entry-time-observable feature that predicts
which cci_divergence trades become end_of_day exits — if there were, that
feature, not exit_reason, would be the real candidate signal):
  - stop-loss distance at entry (% of entry price): end_of_day mean=0.423%
    (n=21) vs other-exit mean=0.391% (n=26) — corr(is_eod, sl_dist_pct)=0.110,
    permutation P=0.487.
  - take-profit distance at entry: end_of_day mean=0.817% vs other=0.759% —
    corr=0.101, permutation P=0.518.
  - direction (BUY/SELL) x is_eod cross-tab: BUY 9/15 eod (60%), SELL 12/32
    eod (37.5%) — a numeric skew exists but was not stability/significance
    tested further since the primary attack already disqualifies the claim;
    noted as the one entry-time-adjacent feature worth a closer look if this
    strategy is ever revisited, but it is direction, which the autopsy
    already tested as its own (non-stable) slice elsewhere.
  - entry hour x is_eod: no clean monotonic pattern (9:00=41%, 10:00=29%,
    11:00=33%, 12:00=50%, 13:00=67%, 14:00=67%, 15:00=50%), all on tiny
    per-hour n (2-17).
  None of the entry-time-observable numeric features predict is_eod above
  noise level.

MULTIPLE-COMPARISONS CONTEXT: the full strategy x exit_reason matrix has 17
cells (not quite the "~20" estimate in the task, but the same order of
magnitude). Permutation test: shuffled exit_reason labels globally 1000x,
looked at the best (highest) avg_pnl among cells with n>=15 (comparable
scale to this cell's n=21) in each permutation. Real best n>=15 cell
avg_pnl = +14.0 (this is literally the cci_divergence/end_of_day cell —
it's the best-performing cell of that size in the real data). Null:
P(best n>=15 cell avg_pnl >= +14.0) = 0.531; null best-cell avg_pnl mean =
+17.5. In other words, a cell at least this good turns up in the majority of
random relabelings — completely unremarkable even before the definitional
problem is considered.

VERDICT: ARTIFACT, and moreover UNUSABLE BY DEFINITION independent of any
statistical result. exit_reason is an outcome label; no entry-time rule can
condition on it. The supporting statistics (no predictive entry-time feature
found, P=0.53 under multiple-comparison permutation) simply confirm there is
nothing here to salvage even as an indirect signal.

CLAIM 4: "Top-3 worst days carry 48.8% of gross losses" (loss clustering)
======================================================================
ATTACKS RUN:
1. Permutation test matched to the actual data-generating structure: shuffled
   the gross_pnl vector against the fixed entry_date vector 1000x (this
   exactly preserves each day's real trade COUNT — 11 trading days, 4 to 22
   trades/day — while breaking any link between calendar day and which
   trades lost). Recomputed top-3-of-11-days gross-loss share each time.
       Null: mean=48.4%, median=48.3%, p90=54.1%, p95=56.2%, max=60.8%
       Real observed: 48.75% (confirms the claimed 48.8%)
       P(null >= real) = 0.455
   The real value sits essentially AT THE MEDIAN of the permutation null.
   Given only 11 trading days and per-day trade counts ranging 4-22 (i.e.
   large variance in how many correlated same-day signals fire together),
   random assignment alone produces 48%+ top-3 concentration nearly half
   the time. The claimed "48.8%" is not evidence of clustering beyond what
   the small-n, correlated-signal structure of this dataset would produce
   by chance anyway.

2. The original autopsy's own stability check used an "even-spread baseline"
   of 3/n_days = 27.3% as the null, which is a strawman: it assumes losses
   would otherwise be uniformly spread across days, ignoring that day-level
   trade counts vary 4-22 and multiple strategies fire on the same signal on
   the same day (the actual source of correlation). Against the CORRECT
   null (permutation preserving real day-level trade-count structure), the
   effect is not stable evidence of clustering — it is close to the
   chance-level baseline for this dataset's actual structure.

3. Top-3 day identification and mechanical-vs-idiosyncratic check:
       2026-07-29: n=22, gross_loss=-1,832.1, win_rate=27.3%,
                   14 SELL / 8 BUY, strategies choppiness_filter(9)+
                   cci_divergence(9)+bb_mean_reversion(4)
       2026-07-21: n=20, gross_loss=-961.2, win_rate=25.0%,
                   14 SELL / 6 BUY, bb_mean_reversion(10)-dominant
       2026-08-03: n=11, gross_loss=-920.7, win_rate=18.2%,
                   8 SELL / 3 BUY, bb_mean_reversion(8)-dominant
   Cross-checked against regime_exit_cache.csv (NIFTY daily closes):
       2026-07-21: NIFTY day return = -0.21%
       2026-07-29: NIFTY day return = +1.10%
       2026-08-03: NOT IN THE CSV (confirmed data gap, already flagged in
                   the original autopsy — genuinely unresolvable from this
                   data source)
   Neither available day is an extreme index move: the full CSV history has
   std=1.30%, so -0.21% and +1.10% are both well inside one standard
   deviation (1.10% is above the 75th percentile of daily moves but nowhere
   near the tails — median=0.06%, max=17.7%, min=-13.0%). These do not look
   like "the market crashed" days. 2026-07-29 in particular is a day NIFTY
   was UP over 1%, while the book was 14-SELL/8-BUY and lost heavily — more
   consistent with an idiosyncratic, strategy-specific bad day (mean-
   reversion/momentum SELL signals fighting a broad rally) than a mechanical,
   unavoidable index-crash loss. 2026-08-03 cannot be checked (CSV gap).

VERDICT: ARTIFACT on the core statistical claim — permutation test puts the
observed 48.8% concentration at the median of what pure chance produces
given this dataset's real day-count structure (P=0.455, nowhere near
significant). The top-3 days are not obviously "mechanical" (large index
moves) either — 2 of 3 checkable days had unremarkable NIFTY moves, and the
3rd is unresolvable due to a genuine data gap already flagged in the
original autopsy.

======================================================================
OVERALL ASSESSMENT
======================================================================
All four "STABLE" candidates fail their designated best attack and are
downgraded to ARTIFACT. Three of the four failures are decisive and
independently corroborating: (1) Wednesday's entire effect is one calendar
date, 2026-07-29, and the autopsy's own "split-half" check for that cell was
mathematically forced to just compare that date against the only other
Wednesday in the sample rather than test anything resembling robustness; (4)
that same date, 2026-07-29, is also the single largest contributor to the
top-3-worst-days claim, and a correctly-specified permutation test (matching
the real per-day trade-count structure, not a strawman uniform-spread
baseline) shows the observed 48.8% concentration is exactly what randomness
produces given only 11 trading days with 4-22 correlated trades each — so
claims 1 and 4 are substantially the same underlying event (one bad trading
day) being counted as two independent "stable patterns." Claim 2 dissolves
under a standard permutation test (P=0.73 on the win-rate gap) and is
additionally shown to be driven by 1-3 outlier stop-losses in one
strategy/symbol rather than a structural BUY/SELL payoff difference. Claim 3
is the cleanest kill of the four: it fails on logical/definitional grounds
before any statistics are needed, because exit_reason is resolved by the
trade's own outcome and cannot be known at entry, so no rule could ever act
on it even if the correlation were bulletproof (it isn't — P=0.53 under a
matched multiple-comparison permutation, and no entry-time feature predicts
it). Combined with the pre-existing base rate of 140+ backtests that have
never beaten buy-and-hold, none of these four candidates clears the bar for
further work; the honest reading of this autopsy is that it correctly
generated hypotheses and its split-half rail correctly flagged most of them
as UNSTABLE/THIN, but the four that were promoted to STABLE survived only
because the rail's chronological-half-split and even-spread-baseline
assumptions happen to be too weak to catch small-calendar-n and correlated-
signal-clustering effects — exactly the failure modes a permutation test
against the real data-generating structure is built to catch, and did.
