PART 1 -- COST (deterministic, exact arithmetic via kite.config.zerodha_charges)
====================================================================================================

Book capital: Rs 100,000. Round-trip cost computed at a flat position
value (buy_value == sell_value == slot size, i.e. isolating cost from any assumed
price move -- the flat components (DP charge, brokerage cap) dominate the small-slot
penalty regardless of P&L, so this isolates the slot-count effect cleanly).

Turnover assumptions: intraday 3 round trips/slot/week (156/yr); delivery/rotation 1 round trip/slot/month (12/yr).

Reading the table: DELIVERY cost as a % of position falls monotonically as N falls,
exactly as the 2026-07-26 audit predicted (bigger slot -> flat DP charge is a smaller %)
-- confirmed here across the full N grid, not just the audit's two example points.

INTRADAY cost as a % of position is FLAT (0.106%) across the whole grid -- this is NOT
a bug. Every intraday charge component here is a pure percentage (STT, exchange, SEBI,
GST, stamp duty); the only flat component is the Rs 20 brokerage cap, and it only binds
once one leg's value exceeds Rs 66,667 (cap / rate). The biggest slot in this grid is Rs 50,000 (N=2), which never reaches that threshold. So the 'flat costs favour
bigger slots' logic from the motivating audit applies to DELIVERY trades in this book's
size range, but NOT to intraday ones -- their only flat lever doesn't engage until a
single slot exceeds ~Rs 66,700, i.e. beyond N=1 on a Rs 1,00,000 book.

Column notes:
  d(cost) bps vs N=5: how many bps/yr of book cost you SAVE by using N instead of 5
    (positive = cheaper than N=5; negative = more expensive than N=5), separately for
    the intraday-turnover and delivery/rotation-turnover assumptions.
  d(vol) pp vs N=5: how many percentage points of ANNUALISED VOL MORE (positive) or
    less (negative) the random N-stock portfolio carries vs N=5, at the median draw.
  bps/vol-pp ('i:'/'d:'): the implied break-even -- bps/yr of cost saved per additional
    percentage point of annualised vol accepted, for intraday and delivery respectively.
    A LOW number means you are giving up a lot of vol for very little cost saving (bad
    trade); a HIGH number means the cost saving is large relative to the vol given up.
    The intraday column is ~0 for every N: as shown in Part 1, intraday cost-as-%-of-
    position is flat across this whole slot-size grid (the brokerage cap doesn't bind
    until ~Rs 66,700/slot), so there is no intraday cost gradient to trade off against
    vol in the first place -- only the delivery column carries a real break-even here.

This is diagnostic, not dispositive, for TWO reasons, not one: (1) a real strategy's
drawdown depends on its own stock selection and exits, which can be better OR worse than
a random breadth-matched portfolio; AND (2) this bootstrap samples the broader
data/daily_universe pool (per the task spec), not the narrower NIFTY-50 data/daily
universe the live strategies actually trade (see the second honesty note above) -- so
these maxDD numbers are plausibly wider than a same-universe comparison would show. The
'BREACHES' flags above should be read as 'a portfolio breadth-diversified this thinly,
drawn from a broad liquid universe, would strain these ceilings' -- not as 'production is
already in breach.' Still: if even this generous/adjacent bound is flirting with a G4
ceiling at low N, that N is a caution flag regardless of any cost saving it offers -- per
the HONESTY REQUIREMENT, no cost saving can justify a slot count that breaches the
contract's risk limits.

====================================================================================================
RECOMMENDATION (research input only -- NOT a production change)
====================================================================================================

Is max_positions=5 defensible? On this evidence: YES, as a reasonable middle point,
with explicit caveats below -- this is not a call to change it, and no change should
happen outside a pre-registered decision process.

1. The cost gradient is real but SMALL in absolute terms across the whole realistic N
   range (2..10): see Part 1 -- the delivery/rotation annualised book cost drag moves by
   well under 1 percentage point across the entire grid, because DP-charge/brokerage-cap
   flatness matters most at very small position sizes, not in the 10k-50k range this
   book actually operates in. Intraday cost drag is larger in absolute %/yr (much higher
   turnover assumed) but is similarly flat-shaped across N.

2. The risk gradient (Part 2) is large and monotonic: fewer names -> materially higher
   annualised vol and materially deeper (more negative) max drawdown, with WIDER
   dispersion across draws at low N (a bad 2-stock draw can be far worse than a bad
   10-stock draw). Going from N=5 to N=2 buys a small cost saving for a large, unevenly
   distributed risk increase -- a poor trade by the numbers here.

3. Going the other way (N=8 or N=10) buys a small amount of extra diversification for a
   small extra cost -- also not obviously worth disturbing the current design for.

4. N=5 sits in the flat part of both curves: cost isn't punishing it relative to N=8/10,
   and it comfortably clears the momo_rotation_63 G4 ceiling (-43.5%) and sits within
   ~1pp of the rsi_trend_confirmation ceiling (-31.4%) under this bound. It does NOT
   clear the tighter cci_divergence (-20.9%) or intraday (-22.5%) ceilings under this
   broad-universe random bound -- see the G4 cross-check. Read correctly (caveats above),
   that is NOT evidence N=5 is unsafe for those two: it means their safety margin comes
   from their specific (narrower, NIFTY-50) universe and stock/entry discipline, not from
   breadth alone -- which is exactly why G4 is judged per-strategy on live paper data,
   not inferred from a generic breadth bound. The defensibility argument for N=5 is that
   it is a stable, unpunished point on the cost curve -- not that breadth alone proves
   every strategy safe; that proof has to come from each strategy's own live record.

CAVEATS (binding):
  - Part 2 measures RANDOM portfolios. It bounds the diversification cost of breadth in
    isolation; it does NOT predict the live strategies' actual risk, which depends on
    their specific stock selection, timing, and correlation with each other and the
    market regime filter. Do not read medians here as forecasts of momo/rsi/cci/intraday
    drawdowns.
  - Part 2 also samples a BROADER universe (data/daily_universe, ~800 liquid NSE stocks,
    per the task spec) than the NIFTY-50-only universe (data/daily, ~48 stocks) the live
    strategies are actually validated and traded on. The vol/drawdown numbers here are
    plausibly an over-estimate of same-universe NIFTY-50 breadth risk -- useful as a wide
    bound, not as a same-universe estimate.
  - Part 1 assumes constant turnover rates (3 RT/slot/week intraday, 1 RT/slot/month
    delivery) uniformly across all N -- real strategies may trade more or less often at
    different slot counts (e.g. a rotation strategy holding fewer, bigger positions may
    also rebalance less often, changing the real trip count, not just the cost per trip).
  - No cost saving computed here can license a slot count whose (random-portfolio-
    bounded) drawdown risk breaches the G4 ceilings in the promotion contract -- see the
    cross-check above. Any future change to max_positions is a pre-registered decision,
    not something this script authorizes.
  - This script makes NO production changes: kite/config.py, max_positions, and all live
    books are untouched.
