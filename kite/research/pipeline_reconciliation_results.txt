WHY deployment fell instead of rising (a real, checkable mechanism, not just "the number moved"):

can't -- and critically, a SKIPPED entry leaves existing, already-invested positions untouched (deployed
capital doesn't get pulled back to cash just because a new entry was rejected). Under 'compounding'
sizing, the target slot = current_equity/5 shrinks IN LOCKSTEP with equity, so during the SAME drawdown
periods the target itself is smaller, and shrink-to-fit no longer has the fixed-slot version's
"binary-reject acts as an accidental risk-off throttle during cash-tight (i.e. losing) stretches" side
effect -- shrink-to-fit instead keeps deploying a compounding-shrunk-but-still-substantial fraction
THROUGH the drawdown. Era1 makes this concrete: baseline -13.35% vs config (A) -20.00% -- fuller,
more consistent exposure through a bad early stretch produced a WORSE outcome, not a better one, because
the underlying stock-picking/timing was genuinely bad in that stretch on Z data, and more consistent
deployment into a bad signal is a real economic loss, not a data or accounting artifact. This is the
single clearest piece of evidence in this whole reconciliation that binary-reject was NOT starving a
good strategy of capital -- if anything, on this data/window, it was accidentally protective.
