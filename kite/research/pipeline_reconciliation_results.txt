MAGNITUDE CHECK -- does price-pipeline divergence explain the CAGR gap, or is something else at play?
====================================================================================================
honest_lab.py (Pipeline Z, COMPOUNDING position sizing): momo lb=63 n=3 regime=True ->
  TRAIN +5.0%/yr, VAL +3.5%/yr (kite/research/lab_results.csv).
rotation_refinement_study.py (Pipeline N, FIXED non-compounding Rs20,000/slot sizing, matching the
  LIVE deployed rules) baseline: full-period -14.560%/yr, Era1 -2.867%, Era2 -8.773%, Era3 -29.588%
  (kite/research/rotation_refinement_results.txt).

Three separate hypotheses, checked against evidence already sitting in this repo:

1. "It's the price data (Z vs N)."
   This script's own step-4 result: only 9.5% of genuinely comparable months (6/63, excluding the
   Z-data-cutoff artifact row) show a DIFFERENT top-3 pick set, and every one of those differs by
   exactly 1 of 3 names, never all 3. If picks were "wildly different portfolios" (the task's opening
   hypothesis), this rate would be much higher. Per-symbol divergence is also a long tail, not a
   systemic problem: 38/48 symbols (79%) diverge by under 2% cumulative over 5.5 years; only 10
   symbols exceed 4%, and only 2 of those (TATAMOTORS, ITC) have a confirmed, well-understood cause
   (demergers outside N's builder-script scope). So: price-pipeline divergence is REAL and CONFIRMED
   for specific names/dates, but it is concentrated and small relative to a strategy-wide -15 to -30
   pp/yr gap -- it cannot be the SOLE or even PRIMARY explanation, though TATAMOTORS's Oct-2025
   demerger lands squarely in Era 3 (the worst era) and is a genuine, non-trivial contributor there.

2. "It's compounding vs fixed-slot position sizing."
   Already tested INSIDE rotation_refinement_study.py itself (its own "DIAGNOSTIC (NON-VERDICT):
   COMPOUNDING SIZING" section, lines ~318-336 of that file) using Pipeline N data both ways:
   FIXED-slot baseline = -14.560%/yr; COMPOUNDING baseline (honest_lab.py's own sizing rule, same N
   data, same costs, same regime filter) = -15.363%/yr -- i.e. compounding does NOT rescue the
   result, it's marginally WORSE. That study's own J12 predictions ("compounding lands near honest_
   lab's positive numbers", "S3-vs-baseline gap collapses under compounding") both explicitly FAILED
   the check the study ran on itself. Sizing convention is RULED OUT as the explanation.

3. "It's the disaster stop / other engine mechanics."
   X0 (no 15% disaster stop) = -14.980%/yr, barely different from baseline's -14.560%/yr -- ruled out
   too, per rotation_refinement_results.txt's own variant table.

By elimination, and consistent with what this script found directly: the dominant remaining
candidate is the PRICE SERIES ITSELF differing between what honest_lab.py validated against
(Pipeline Z) and what rotation_refinement_study.py actually ran on (Pipeline N) for the specific
names/dates where they disagree, PLUS cost-convention differences between the two scripts (J4 in

not a single smoking gun -- see VERDICT below.

VERDICT
====================================================================================================
MIXED -- neither pipeline is "cleanly right" and the CAGR gap does not reduce to one root cause.

1. Pipeline N (bhavcopy + data/corp_actions_adjustments.csv) has a CONFIRMED, SYSTEMATIC blind spot:
   build_corp_actions.py only parses split and bonus corporate-action subjects. Demergers (confirmed:
   TATAMOTORS Oct-2025, ITC Jan-2025) are invisible to it -- not flagged as NaN/unparseable (which
   would at least HALT the study), just silently absent, because is_relevant_candidate() never
   matches demerger subject text at all. Pipeline Z (Zerodha) appears to correctly back-adjust for
   both of these real events (confirmed by comparing raw OHLC prints: Z's post-event bars are
   byte-identical to N's raw post-event bars, while Z's pre-event bars are scaled down by a
   plausible-looking factor that N's never applies). This is a real, fixable, well-scoped bug in
   Pipeline N's data pipeline, not a philosophical disagreement.
2. Pipeline N ALSO has an unresolved, smaller, unexplained residual: TATASTEEL's slow ~15% drift with
   no discrete cause, plus isolated single-day disagreements in BPCL/COALINDIA/RELIANCE that this
   repo's data cannot resolve either way. These could be N-side bhavcopy print errors, Z-side feed
   quirks, or genuine but differently-measured market moves -- undetermined without an external
   primary source.
3. The MAGNITUDE of the honest_lab.py-vs-rotation_refinement_study.py CAGR gap is NOT explained by
   price-pipeline divergence alone (pick-set divergence is only ~9.5% of months, mostly 1-of-3 names)
   NOR by position-sizing convention (rotation_refinement_study.py's own compounding diagnostic ran
   the test and it failed) NOR by the disaster-stop rule (X0 barely differs from baseline). Some
   combination of (a) the confirmed demerger-handling gap in N, concentrated in Era 3 where it does
   real damage, (b) the unresolved smaller data disagreements, and (c) cost-convention differences
   between the two scripts (J4: entry-cost haircut + flat DP charge in rotation_refinement_study.py
   vs exit-only fee settlement in honest_lab.py) together produce the gap. No single item in this
   list is large enough by itself; this script cannot further apportion the remainder with the data
   on hand.
4. Which number better reflects reality: NEITHER, as currently computed, should be trusted at face
   value. honest_lab.py's +3.5-5.0%/yr is validated on Pipeline Z, which handles at least the two
   demerger cases correctly (a real strength) but was never cross-checked against N's independently-
   built corp-action table, and (being Zerodha's black box) its adjustment methodology cannot be
   audited from this repo alone. rotation_refinement_study.py's negative numbers are computed on
   Pipeline N, which has a CONFIRMED, non-trivial data defect (missing demerger adjustments) that
   inflates its losses in a way that is real but not fully quantified, layered under different
   (also real, also deliberate -- see rotation_refinement_study.py's J1/J4) cost and sizing
   conventions that independently make it a stricter, more LIVE-faithful test than honest_lab.py's
   compounding walk-forward. The honest reading: rotation_refinement_study.py's NEGATIVE verdict on
   the LIVE-deployed strategy variant should NOT be waved away as "just a data bug" -- most of its
   verdict-bearing comparisons (S3 vs baseline, S5 vs baseline, X0 vs baseline) are RELATIVE
   comparisons where both sides share the same N-pipeline data and the same demerger blind spot, so
   those FAIL verdicts survive this audit largely intact. What does NOT survive intact is the
   ABSOLUTE baseline CAGR level (-14.56%/yr, -29.6%/yr era3) -- that number is inflated (made more
   negative) by at least the TATAMOTORS/ITC demerger gap, by an amount this script cannot precisely
   quantify without rebuilding Pipeline N's corp-action table to include demerger handling and
   rerunning.
